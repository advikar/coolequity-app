# Bakersfield data audit · 2026-10-12

Scope: every field in `cities/bakersfield/data/bakersfield.geojson` (4,054 areas, 3,767
residential, data version `20260921-stability`), its inputs, the ward roll-up, and the figures
the app, chooser and guide quote. Method: a script of 41 checks (schema, ranges, internal
consistency, independent score recomputation, geometry, wards, cool places) plus a cross-check
of the headline population and age figures against the Census. Result: **every check passes;
17 are informational.** (One check first flagged the 62 greenness stand-in cells because it
expected a canopy ratio; restricted to canopy-source cells it passes with a 0.01-point deviation.)

## Verdict

The data is real, internally consistent, and the app represents it accurately, with three
qualifications that should be stated more plainly than they are today (section 3).

## 2. Figures worth knowing

- Top of the ranking under the recommended mix: Village at Lakeside E 7 (52.2 °C, 2.6% cover,
  503 residents, Ward 1), then Bakersfield S 3 and S 5. All 25 of the top 25 are on the 2022
  aerial canopy source.
- City-wide street capacity inside the boundary: 481,765 tree positions at 10 m spacing on
  both sides; the default 25% scenario across every cell would be about 120,000 trees, about
  $60M at $500.
- By ward: Ward 2 carries the highest mean need and the most top-100 areas.

## 3. Three qualifications to state more plainly

**a. 39% of residential cells use a legacy canopy model, not the 2022 aerial survey.** 1,459
cells (20,822 residents, 5% of the total) carry `canopy_source = chm-legacy`, a 2009 to 2020
height model with unknown assessed extent, and 62 more (2,532 residents) use a satellite
greenness stand-in on the 0–100 index. The app marks both per cell, the "Aerial coverage ≥99%"
filter isolates the clean cells, and the guide explains it. The README row for Bakersfield
says "USFS/CAL FIRE 2022 aerial" without qualification. A few legacy cells along the Kern River
read 95 to 99% canopy with two residents each (orchard or riparian cover); they cannot rank.
*Suggested wording:* "2022 aerial tree cover on 61% of areas (94% of residents); an older height
model or a greenness stand-in elsewhere, marked in each area."

**b. Walking times are long and most carry a review flag.** Median walk to the nearest mapped
cool place is 61 minutes; 77% of cells are flagged `approach-review` because the cell centre
sits more than 100 m from the walking network. The arithmetic is right and the flag is honest;
the guide should say how common it is. Walking carries zero weight in the recommended mix.

**c. A/C is nearly uniform** (3.5-point spread) and this build already gives it zero weight,
with the "Homes without A/C" preset to turn it on. Nothing to change; worth keeping.

## 4. What this audit cannot verify

Landsat surface temperatures and the USFS canopy classification are taken as published;
OpenStreetMap completeness for streets and cool places is unverified; the within-block-group
placement of residents is a model (block-group totals reconcile). The cheapest next step is a
ground-truth walk of the top five cells with a thermometer and a camera.
